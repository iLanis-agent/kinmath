Kin Math

A shared ancestor 3 generations above you and 2 above them makes them your 1st cousin once removed, 1 generation older than you.

Boundary: arithmetic only Kin Math names a relationship from the generation counts you type, using the common English genealogy convention, and counts ancestors by doubling. It does not know your family tree, anyone's DNA, other cultures' kinship terms, or any legal standing such as inheritance, marriage or citizenship, and it gives no verdict on whether a relationship exists or is allowed. Every number is your own entry.

Starting examples: a shared ancestor 3 generations above you and 2 above them, the 3rd generation back, and 5 generations back at 25 years each from a birth in 1990.

The kin truth

Counting 3 generations up from you and 2 generations up from them to the shared ancestor: they are your 1st cousin once removed, 1 generation older than you.

The ancestor truth

Generation 3 back (great-grandparents): at most 8 ancestors in that generation and 14 counting every generation up to it, if no ancestor is counted twice.

The era truth

Counting 5 generations back at 25 years each from a birth in 1990 reaches the year 1865, 125 years earlier.
